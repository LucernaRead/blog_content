You open a Spanish novel, recognise the first few words, and spend the rest of the paragraph in a dictionary. The story disappears behind the work of decoding it. A graded reader gives you a smaller step: a complete story written or adapted for a stated level.

If you are choosing your first Spanish graded reader, start with **the exact edition, a sample, and a story you want to finish**. The A2 adaptation and the original can be very different books — and Spanish readers are published on both sides of the Atlantic, in different varieties of Spanish.

---

## What is a Spanish graded reader?

Graded readers control language for learners. Some are new stories; others adapt existing works. A1, A2 and B1 labels help you find a starting point, but no label guarantees every book at that level feels equally easy: vocabulary, topic and interest all matter.

A children's book is not automatically a graded reader, and a short original novel is not automatically beginner Spanish: neither length nor intended age tells you how much idiomatic language a text assumes.

## Use a ten-minute sample test

This is a practical selection method, not a CEFR assessment. Read a sample for ten minutes. Leave the dictionary closed on the first pass, except when a missing word makes the action impossible to follow. Then ask:

- Who is involved, what happened, and what might happen next?
- Can I describe the passage in a couple of sentences?
- Am I curious enough to read another page?

If you can follow the events but miss details, the book may be a useful stretch. If you cannot tell what happened without translating most sentences, try an easier sample. If you understand comfortably but dislike the story, change the story rather than forcing yourself through it.

## Paper, EPUB or PDF: check before you buy

Choose paper if that is the format you will actually use. For digital reading, ask whether you get a downloadable file or only access inside a seller's app: many Spanish graded readers come as a book-plus-app package whose audio and activities live in the publisher's app, and a digital edition does not necessarily provide an EPUB or PDF you can import elsewhere. Check format and DRM before buying specifically for Lucerna.

For a PDF, check that its text is selectable: a page image is not searchable text, and a scanned PDF will not offer the same lookups as a text-based ebook.

A classic being out of copyright does not make a modern adaptation or recording free to redistribute; publisher samples and library loans are the way to explore a title without confusing a preview with a free book.

## Turn one paragraph into a reading session

Here is an original practice passage written for this guide, not an excerpt from a recommended book and not an officially graded text:

> Marta llega a la estación. Su tren sale en diez minutos, pero no encuentra su billete. Busca en su bolso. Por fin, lo encuentra debajo de un libro.

Read for the action first: Marta reaches the station, cannot find her ticket, searches her bag, and finds it under a book. If **billete** blocks the scene, look it up. If the last sentence is unclear, ask about **lo**: here it refers to the ticket. Then read the sentence again.

## When should you try an original novel?

No level makes every original suddenly easy. After finishing an adaptation, sample an original that interests you: familiarity with the plot helps, but the original brings its own language and style.

Keep a comfortable graded reader alongside it for momentum. If the original turns every session into sentence-by-sentence translation, put it aside and return later. That is a choice about the book, not a verdict on your Spanish.

## Questions before you choose

### Can I start a Spanish graded reader at A1?

Try an A1-labelled sample with the test above: A1 books are a starting point, not a promise someone with no Spanish will understand every page.

### Is Don Quixote an A2 book?

The same series lists an adaptation of *Don Quixote* at A2 — but that label belongs to the adaptation, not to Cervantes' original. Do not treat a level attached to an adaptation as a universal label for the work: check the exact edition and read a sample.
