You open a German novel, recognise the first few words, and spend the rest of the paragraph in a dictionary. The verb arrives at the end, and the story disappears behind the work of decoding it. A graded reader gives you a smaller step: a complete story written or adapted for a stated level.

If you are choosing your first German graded reader, start with **the exact edition, a sample, and a story you want to finish**. The B1 adaptation and the original can be very different books — and for German classics, the same title often circulates as a school edition, an older adaptation and the unabridged original at once.

---

## What is a German graded reader?

Graded readers control language for learners. Some are new stories; others adapt existing works. A1, A2 and B1 labels help you find a starting point, but no label guarantees every book at that level feels equally easy.

A children's book is not automatically a graded reader, and a short original novel is not automatically beginner German: neither length nor intended age tells you how much idiomatic language a text assumes.

| A1 | Black Cat–Cideb, Lesen und Üben, [Von Hexen und Prinzessinnen](https://www.blackcat-cideb.com/en/books/von-hexen-und-prinzessinnen-5) | Four well-known Brothers Grimm tales — *Schneewittchen*, *Hänsel und Gretel*, *Frau Holle* and *König Drosselbart*. | B1 | Black Cat–Cideb, Lesen und Üben, [Die Verwandlung](https://www.blackcat-cideb.com/en/books/die-verwandlung) | Kafka's story, adapted: Gregor Samsa wakes transformed into a huge insect. The publisher lists this edition at B1 (exam reference: ZD B1) — a genuine literary work, in a version built for learners. |

The list is deliberately short: compare samples at the same level before jumping a level, and choose the story you want to return to tomorrow over the title you feel you ought to read.

**Check the series name, level and ISBN before ordering.** Searching for “Die Verwandlung” can lead to the original, a translation, a school edition or an adaptation — and bookseller listings for older printings of this series do not always carry the same level label as the publisher's current catalogue. A level printed on one edition does not apply to every edition of that work. Check availability with the publisher, your bookseller or your library.

## Use a ten-minute sample test

This is a practical selection method, not a CEFR assessment. Read a sample for ten minutes, dictionary closed on the first pass, except when a missing word makes the action impossible to follow. Then ask:

- Who is involved, what happened, and what might happen next?
- Can I describe the passage in a couple of sentences?
- Am I curious enough to read another page?

If you can follow the events but miss details, the book may be a useful stretch. If you cannot tell what happened without translating most sentences, try an easier sample. If you understand comfortably but dislike the story, change the story.

## Paper, EPUB or PDF: check before you buy

Choose paper if that is the format you will actually use. For digital reading, ask whether you get a downloadable file or only access inside a seller's or publisher's app: a digital edition does not necessarily provide an EPUB or PDF you can import elsewhere. ## Turn one paragraph into a reading session

Here is an original practice passage written for this guide, not an excerpt from a recommended book and not an officially graded text:

> Anna kommt am Bahnhof an. Ihr Zug fährt in zehn Minuten, aber sie findet ihre Fahrkarte nicht. Sie sucht in ihrer Tasche. Endlich findet sie sie unter einem Buch.

Read for the action first: Anna arrives at the station, cannot find her ticket, searches her bag, and finds it under a book. If **Fahrkarte** blocks the scene, look it up. If the last sentence is unclear, look at the second **sie**: it refers to the ticket — *die Fahrkarte* is feminine, so the ticket is “her” in German grammar, not a person. Then read the sentence again.

## When should you try an original novel?

No level makes every original suddenly easy. After finishing an adaptation, sample an original that interests you: familiarity with the plot helps, but the original brings its own language and style. Kafka is the clearest case — the original *Die Verwandlung* is a step beyond its B1 adaptation, even though the story is the same.

Keep a comfortable graded reader alongside it. If the original turns every session into sentence-by-sentence translation, put it aside and return later. That is a choice about the book, not a verdict on your German.

## Questions before you choose

### Can I start a German graded reader at A1?

Try an A1-labelled sample with the test above: A1 books are a starting point, not a promise someone with no German will understand every page.

### Is Die Verwandlung a B1 book?

The publisher's current catalogue lists its *Lesen und Üben* adaptation at B1 — but that label belongs to the adaptation, not to Kafka's original, and older listings do not always agree. Check the exact edition and read a sample.
