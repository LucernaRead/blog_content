We overestimate what we can read in a weekend and underestimate what we can read in a year. The marathon session — five hours with a dictionary and a fresh resolution — feels like progress, but it rarely survives contact with a second weekend. The reader who turns one page a night, on the other hand, finishes a shelf by spring and barely notices the climb.

## The arithmetic of small numbers

Five pages a night is about fifteen minutes. Over a year that is more than eighteen hundred pages — six or seven novels in a language you are still learning. Nobody plans to read six novels. You simply keep showing up, and the books accumulate behind you like footprints.

The marathon has the opposite problem. Five intense hours is a lot to schedule, easy to skip, and exhausting enough that your brain quietly files reading under “work.” What gets scheduled once gets cancelled once. What happens nightly becomes part of the furniture.

## Why daily beats intense

A language is not a wall you scale and then stand on top of; it is a garden that wants regular tending. Vocabulary fades on a predictable curve, and the cheapest way to fight that curve is to meet the same words again tomorrow, in a slightly different sentence, before they have a chance to leave. Daily reading is spaced repetition that doesn’t feel like studying.

> You don’t find time to read. You spend a little, nightly, until the spending becomes the habit.

## Making it stick

The trick is to make the habit smaller than your resistance to it. A few things help:

- Anchor it to something you already do every night — after you brush your teeth, before you turn off the lamp.
- Keep the bar embarrassingly low. “One page” is a promise you can keep on a bad day; most nights you’ll read more.
- Read the same place, same time. Decisions are expensive; routines are free.
- Choose a book you actually want to finish, not the one you think you should be reading.

Do that, and fluency stops being a project you have to find energy for. It becomes the thing that happens while you were just reading a story.
