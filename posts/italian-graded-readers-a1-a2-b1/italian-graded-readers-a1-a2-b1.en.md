You open an Italian novel and within a page you are drowning in verb forms. Every third sentence sends you to a dictionary, the story never gets moving, and the book goes back on the shelf. That is not a verdict on your Italian. It usually means the step was too big.

A graded reader is the smaller step: a complete story, written or adapted for a stated level, with vocabulary and grammar controlled so a learner can finish it. For Italian, choosing well means knowing one honest fact up front: the ladder is split. A1 lives with Alma Edizioni, and A2–B1 live with Black Cat–Cideb. This guide explains that split, gives you three editions to start with, and shows you how to test a book before you buy it.

---

## What is an Italian graded reader?

An Italian graded reader is a book written for learners, not native speakers. Its language is controlled: sentence length, verb tenses, and vocabulary stay within what a learner at a stated CEFR level — A1, A2, or B1 — can handle. Some are original stories written at that level; others are adapted classics, shortened and simplified.

That control is the point. A children's book is not automatically beginner Italian: it can use playful, idiomatic language a learner has never met. A short original novel is not automatically easy either. A graded reader promises something specific — this text was built for this level — checkable against the publisher's level scheme.

## Three editions to start your search

| Level | Edition to look for | Why consider it |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Alma Edizioni, Letture Italiano Facile, Livello 1, "Dov'è Yukio?" by Alessandro De Giuli, book + CD, 64 pages | A genuine A1 start, at the level labelled "elementare" |
| A2 | Black Cat–Cideb, Imparare Leggendo, Livello 1 (A2), "Indagine a Firenze" | A detective story set in Florence, at the ladder's first level |
| B1 | Black Cat–Cideb, Imparare Leggendo, Livello 2 (B1), "Mistero a Roma" | A mystery set in Rome, for when A2 feels comfortable |

Why does this table cross publishers? Because Imparare Leggendo starts at A2. Per the publisher, its four levels run Livello 1 = A2, Livello 2 = B1, Livello 3 = B2, and Livello 4 = C1 — there is no A1 rung. That differs from the French, German, and Spanish Black Cat ladders, which start at A1. So A1 comes from a different shelf: Alma's Letture Italiano Facile, whose same series and level also offers "Amore e cappuccino" by Valeria Blasi.

One caution from our other language guides: a level printed on one edition does not transfer to every edition of a work. Check the series, level, and ISBN before ordering, because bookseller listings for older printings may carry different labels.

Bonus at A2: Black Cat's "La Bella Italia" offers non-fiction books about Italian regions and cities, at A2 in the publisher's scheme.

## Use a ten-minute sample test

A level label is a starting point, not a guarantee a book fits you. Use this practical test — a reading method, not a CEFR assessment. Open a sample and read for ten minutes, dictionary closed on the first pass.

Then ask: Who is involved? What happened? What might happen next?

If you follow the events but miss details, the book is a useful stretch. If you are translating most sentences word by word just to keep up, go easier — drop a level or pick a simpler title. If you are comfortable but bored, the level may be right and the story wrong: change the story first.

## Turn one paragraph into a reading session

One paragraph, read well, is enough for a session. Here is an original practice passage, written for this guide:

> Marco arriva in albergo nel pomeriggio. Ha prenotato una camera piccola con vista sul mare. Lascia la valigia sul letto e apre la finestra: l'aria sa di sale e di pane caldo.

First, read for the action: Marco arrives at a hotel in the afternoon, leaves his suitcase on the bed, and opens the window. Then choose which unknown word is worth looking up. Here, "prenotato" blocks the meaning if you miss it — he has booked the room. One word is a classic false friend: "la camera" here means the (hotel) room, not a camera.

## When should you try an original novel?

After B1 readers feel manageable, sample an original novel you genuinely want to read, using the same ten-minute test. Keep a comfortable graded reader alongside it, so a hard book is never your only reading. If the original is still too much, stepping back is a choice about that book, not your Italian — return to it later.

## Questions before you choose

### Can I start at A1 with no Italian?

Try an A1 sample with the ten-minute test above. A1 is a starting point for beginners, not a promise it will feel easy on day one — if you can follow who is involved and what happens, you can begin.

### Why does the Black Cat Italian series start at A2?

That is simply how the publisher built this ladder: Imparare Leggendo runs from A2 to C1. It is why our A1 pick comes from Alma's Letture Italiano Facile instead.

### Is "Mistero a Roma" a B1 book?

The publisher lists Livello 2 at B1, and "Mistero a Roma" is a Livello 2 title. Check the exact edition, since labels can differ between printings.

### How many graded readers should I finish before an original novel?
